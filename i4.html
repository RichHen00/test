The Boyhood Yearning for a Lost Horizon

Young grew up where the Atlantic batters the Cornish cliffs. In a landscape of half-lost churches and shipwrecks, a childhood fascination with the horizon became a mandate.

It was a simple idea: take a series of ships of many sizes and kinds; go where they lead for a few months; see what happens. It was an adaptation of the old idea of Running Away to Sea, a boyhood yearning bred of tales, marvellous tales Of ships and stars and isles where good men rest.

No doubt my dream of the sea was born during the long summers I spent as a child in what I still think of as the oldest-feeling, most soul-subduing and cosily creepy part of England. This is the Wreckers' Coast: a place of buzzards and seals and effigies of knights in dim, half-lost churches, where seas pound into cliffbound bays that have swallowed seamen from a hundred wrecked schooners.

— Slow Boats to China
